# Fundamentals of Quantum Information and Computing Systems
Document ID: QC-INTRO-2026
Research Group: Advanced Quantum Computing Laboratory

1. Core Principles: Qubits, Superposition, and Entanglement
1.1 While classical bits exist deterministically as either 0 or 1, quantum bits (qubits) exist as linear combinations of orthogonal states |0> and |1>, mathematically formulated as |psi> = alpha|0> + beta|1>, where |alpha|^2 + |beta|^2 = 1.
1.2 Superposition permits an n-qubit register to simultaneously evaluate 2^n states until measurement collapses the wavefunction.
1.3 Quantum Entanglement creates non-local correlations between distinct qubits such that the quantum state of one cannot be described independently of the state of the other, even when separated by arbitrary spatial distances.

2. Milestone Quantum Algorithms
2.1 Shor's Algorithm: A polynomial-time quantum algorithm for integer factorization and discrete logarithms. It factorizes an N-bit integer in O((log N)^2 (log log N) (log log log N)) time, demonstrating exponential speedup over classical algorithms and posing a theoretical threat to RSA-2048 encryption.
2.2 Grover's Algorithm: Provides a quadratic speedup for unstructured database searching and black-box function inversion, reducing search complexity from O(N) to O(sqrt(N)).

3. Quantum Error Correction & Fault Tolerance
3.1 Physical qubits suffer from decoherence and environmental noise caused by thermal fluctuations and electromagnetic interference.
3.2 Fault-tolerant quantum computing utilizes topological Surface Codes, where approximately 1,000 physical superconducting qubits are combined to synthesize a single fault-tolerant logical qubit with an error threshold below 10^-3 per gate operation.
